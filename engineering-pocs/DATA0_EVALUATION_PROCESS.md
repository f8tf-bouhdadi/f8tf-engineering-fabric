# DATA0 Custom Engineering Engagement Assessment Process

This reference describes how F8TF assesses a submitted DATA0 Custom
Engineering Engagement request.

This process applies only when the standard qualified DATA0 delivery does not
fully cover the prospective client's intended context.

It is not required to review, obtain or verify the standard qualified DATA0
delivery.

Submission of a request does not create a contract and does not automatically
grant access to a client-specific delivery.

When an evaluation proceeds, the concrete engineering activities, outputs,
verification, delivery and acceptance are governed by:

[DATA0 Engineering Service Execution Process](DATA0_SERVICE_EXECUTION_PROCESS.md)

## 1. Request Received

The submitted request identifies:

- the requested engagement;
- the client need;
- the bounded evaluation scenario;
- the target environment;
- the guarantees to evaluate;
- the proposed acceptance criteria;
- and any additional non-confidential constraints.

## 2. Initial Review

F8TF reviews whether the request contains enough information to understand:

- the engineering problem;
- the expected result;
- the declared environment;
- the requested DATA0 behavior;
- and the proposed acceptance conditions.

Possible outcomes are:

- sufficient information;
- clarification required;
- or request outside the publicly declared DATA0 scope.

## 3. Fit Assessment

The request is compared with the current DATA0 Engineering Service:

- service scope;
- engineering guarantees;
- current qualification;
- verification evidence;
- and declared limitations.

The assessment may conclude that:

- DATA0-ES is sufficient for the bounded need;
- DATA0-ES requires client-specific adaptation;
- DATA0-ES must be composed with complementary Engineering Services;
- or the requested need is outside the currently qualified scope.

## 4. Engagement Decision

Depending on the assessment, F8TF may propose:

- an initial technical clarification;
- a controlled evaluation delivery;
- a scoped Engineering PoC;
- a client-specific adaptation;
- an integration discussion;
- a commercial proposal;
- or no engagement when the need cannot be truthfully supported.

## 5. Scope Definition

Before a controlled evaluation or PoC begins, the parties must identify:

- the target Engineering Service;
- the bounded scenario;
- the client and supplier responsibilities;
- the applicable guarantees;
- the declared exclusions;
- the verification actions;
- the evidence to be produced;
- the acceptance criteria;
- and the expected conclusion.

## 6. Delivery Decision

The public standard qualified DATA0 delivery remains available at no cost and
independently of this Custom Engineering Engagement process.

When an engagement requires an additional client-specific DATA0 delivery,
that delivery is prepared only after the applicable evaluation scope and
delivery conditions have been established.

A delivery must be:

- identified;
- versioned;
- integrity-verifiable;
- accompanied by its applicable documentation;
- accompanied by qualification evidence;
- and explicit about its limitations.

## 7. Client Verification

The client may:

- verify package integrity;
- inspect the supplied evidence;
- execute the documented verification procedure;
- compare observed results with the declared guarantees;
- and record deviations.

## 8. Acceptance Decision

The client may conclude:

- accepted;
- accepted with conditions;
- revision required;
- or rejected.

The decision should identify the evaluated delivery, the applied criteria,
the observed results and any unresolved conditions.

## 9. Commercial Continuation

A successful evaluation may lead to:

- a scoped PoC;
- an adaptation engagement;
- an integration engagement;
- a commercial delivery;
- support;
- or composition with other F8TF Engineering Services.

No commercial engagement exists until its scope and terms are explicitly
agreed.
