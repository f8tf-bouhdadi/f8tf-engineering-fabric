# F8TF Guarantees

F8TF uses explicit guarantees to make engineering claims clearer, bounded and
verifiable.

A guarantee describes an expected property of an Engineering Capability,
Product or PoC within a defined scope.

## Why Guarantees Matter

Organizations need more than broad statements such as secure, reliable or
compliant.

They need to know:

- what is guaranteed;
- under which conditions;
- for which scope;
- how the guarantee is verified;
- and which evidence supports it.

## F8TF Guarantee Principles

F8TF follows four principles:

1. No claim without verification.
2. No verification without evidence.
3. No evidence without traceability and reproducibility.
4. No guarantee beyond its declared scope and maturity.

## Current Guarantee Areas

### Trusted Distributed Communication

Examples include:

- explicit trust boundaries;
- policy-constrained bindings;
- declared communication requirements;
- traceable binding outcomes.

### Governed Service Discovery

Examples include:

- immutable publication;
- deterministic discovery and selection;
- explicit rejection outcomes;
- traceable publication and selection evidence.

### Traceable Engineering Data

Examples include:

- deterministic persistence;
- versioned information;
- idempotent operations;
- reproducible audit queries;
- evidence preservation.

## Guarantee Maturity

Guarantees are presented according to their current maturity.

They may be:

- demonstrated by a runnable scenario;
- specified and audit-ready;
- prepared for formal proving;
- or formally justified.

F8TF does not present target maturity as completed assurance.

## Evidence

Guarantees are supported by verification actions and identifiable evidence
appropriate to their scope.

Evidence may include:

- execution reports;
- manifests;
- integrity hashes;
- acceptance results;
- traceability records;
- and client-verifiable packages.
