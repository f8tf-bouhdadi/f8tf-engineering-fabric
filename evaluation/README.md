# F8TF Engineering Service Evaluation

F8TF Engineering Services are defined through explicit Service Contracts,
declared guarantees, qualification boundaries and independently verifiable
engineering evidence.

Evaluation starts from the published Engineering Service definition.

The purpose of evaluation is to determine whether an existing qualified
Engineering Service satisfies a prospective client's bounded need.

---

## 1. Start from an Engineering Service

Before requesting an evaluation, review the Engineering Service that addresses
your need.

For each service, examine:

- the Customer Need;
- the Engineering Promise;
- the Service Contract;
- the declared Service Guarantees;
- the current qualification status;
- the declared limitations;
- and the available delivery conditions.

[Browse F8TF Engineering Services](../products/)

---

## 2. Standard Evaluation

Some F8TF Engineering Services provide a publicly available qualified delivery.

When a public qualified delivery is available, you can evaluate the service
directly from the published qualified delivery.

The standard evaluation path is:

1. review the published Engineering Service;
2. obtain the identified qualified delivery;
3. verify the delivery integrity;
4. inspect the supplied qualification evidence;
5. execute the documented independent verification procedure;
6. operate or evaluate the delivered realization within its declared scope;
7. compare the observed behaviour with the published Service Contract and
   guarantees.

A standard evaluation does not require a client-specific scenario or new
acceptance criteria.

The applicable scope, guarantees and limitations are those already declared by
the qualified Engineering Service.

---

## 3. Controlled Evaluation

A controlled evaluation may be appropriate when:

- the required Runtime Delivery is not publicly distributed;
- the intended environment requires additional assessment;
- the prospective client's need is not fully covered by the standard qualified
  scope;
- additional acceptance criteria must be considered;
- or several Engineering Services may need to be composed.

A controlled evaluation request should identify, without confidential
information where possible:

- the target Engineering Service;
- the engineering need;
- the bounded evaluation scenario;
- the target environment;
- the guarantees of interest;
- the proposed acceptance criteria;
- and relevant constraints.

---

## 4. Fit Assessment

F8TF compares the requested need with the selected Engineering Service:

- Service Contract;
- declared scope;
- qualified guarantees;
- qualification status;
- verification evidence;
- delivery conditions;
- and declared limitations.

The assessment may conclude that:

- the existing qualified Engineering Service is sufficient;
- a controlled evaluation delivery is appropriate;
- or the requested need is outside the currently qualified scope.

F8TF does not claim support for a requirement that cannot be justified by the
declared service scope and available engineering evidence.

---

## 5. Independent Verification

Evaluation is evidence-driven.

Depending on the Engineering Service and delivery type, a recipient may be able
to:

- verify package integrity;
- inspect Service Contract and guarantee evidence;
- verify the qualified delivery or runtime identity;
- execute documented verification procedures;
- compare observed results with declared guarantees;
- and record deviations.

The exact verification procedure is defined by the applicable Engineering
Service and delivery.

---

## 6. Possible Next Steps

An evaluation may conclude that the selected Engineering Service satisfies the
intended bounded need, does not satisfy it, or requires further technical
evaluation.

For further engineering discussion, use GitHub Discussions.
